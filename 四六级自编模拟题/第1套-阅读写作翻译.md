## 一、仔细阅读 Passage 1（科技 / 社会）

**文章（约 420 词）**

The promise of the "paperless office" has been made for decades, yet the average office worker still prints thousands of pages a year. The reason is not stubbornness but something subtler: paper carries a set of cognitive advantages that screens have not managed to replace. Psychologists who study reading have repeatedly found that people comprehend and remember text better when it is read on paper, an effect that grows stronger as the text becomes longer and more demanding.

One widely accepted explanation points to the physical layout of a page. When we read a book or a printout, our minds build a rough "map" of where each idea sits—top left, halfway down, near the fold. This spatial memory acts as a scaffold for the information itself, making it easier to retrieve later. A screen, by contrast, offers no fixed geography. Text scrolls away the moment it is read, leaving the reader with little sense of where anything was. The brain, it seems, treats location as part of the memory.

A second factor is the way attention behaves in each medium. Screens are engineered for interruption: notifications, hyperlinks and the ever-present option to switch tasks constantly invite the reader to drift. Even when no notification arrives, the mere possibility of one is enough to consume a share of working memory—an effect researchers call "attentional residue." Paper, being inert, asks nothing of the reader. It is a technology whose single purpose is to be read, and that absence of competing demands is precisely what allows deeper focus.

The consequences are not merely nostalgic. Several studies of students have shown that those who take notes by hand, or who read assigned material on paper, consistently outperform peers who type or read on screens—not because they are cleverer, but because the slower, more deliberate process forces them to summarise rather than transcribe, and to think rather than skim. Some firms that once rushed to digitise every workflow have quietly kept paper for the few tasks that demand judgment: legal review, financial sign-off, the final read of a contract.

This is not an argument for abandoning digital tools, which remain unmatched for searching, sharing and storing information at scale. It is, rather, a reminder that media are not neutral containers. Each shapes the way we attend, remember and decide. The sensible question is therefore not "paper or screen?" but "paper or screen, for this particular task, for this particular reader?"—and the honest answer is that both still deserve a place on the modern desk.

**Q1.** What is the main point of the passage?
A. The paperless office is an outdated ideal that modern firms should abandon.
B. Paper retains cognitive advantages that screens have not yet replaced.
C. Digital tools are superior to paper for every kind of office work.
D. Students should stop using screens entirely when they study.

**答案 B**　解析：全文围绕"纸张在认知层面仍有优势"展开——首段点题，后三段用空间记忆、注意力、学生成绩三个角度论证，末段收束为"按任务选择媒介"。B 最准确。A 与末段"不是要抛弃数字工具"相反；C 过度；D 极端化。
**易错点**：末段让步句"digital tools remain unmatched"易被误读成作者倒向数字工具，注意让步后的落脚点（both deserve a place）。

**Q2.** Why does the author mention the "map" that readers build when reading on paper?
A. To show that reading is fundamentally a visual activity.
B. To explain how spatial memory helps information retrieval.
C. To argue that screens should be redesigned to feel like paper.
D. To prove that long texts are impossible to read on screens.

**答案 B**　解析：第二段用"mental map / spatial memory"解释纸媒优势的机制——位置感成为记忆支架，便于日后提取。A 偷换概念；C、D 原文未提或极端化。
**易错点**："map"是比喻，落点是 spatial memory 支撑 retrieval，勿只抓字面选 A。

**Q3.** What does "attentional residue" most likely refer to?
A. The habit of checking notifications while working.
B. The memory left behind after finishing a difficult task.
C. The mental cost of knowing a task could be interrupted.
D. The difficulty of returning to work after a long break.

**答案 C**　解析：第三段解释——即使无通知，"mere possibility of one is enough to consume working memory"，即"可能被干扰"本身占用认知资源。C 吻合。A 是行为；B、D 偷换对象。
**易错点**：residue 易被字面理解成"任务没做完的残留"，实为"中断可能性带来的持续认知占用"。

**Q4.** According to the passage, why do students who take notes by hand tend to do better?
A. They are naturally more intelligent than their peers.
B. They spend more time reading the same material.
C. They are forced to summarise and think rather than merely transcribe.
D. They avoid using digital devices, which are inherently distracting.

**答案 C**　解析：末段第四句明确——"not because cleverer, but because the slower, more deliberate process forces them to summarise rather than transcribe, and to think rather than skim"。C 为同义改写。
**易错点**：作者明确排除"聪明"（not cleverer），A 是典型干扰项。

**Q5.** What does the author recommend in the final paragraph?
A. Replace paper with digital tools as quickly as possible.
B. Choose the medium according to the task and the reader.
C. Use paper only for tasks that involve signing documents.
D. Keep paper and screens strictly separate in the office.

**答案 B**　解析：末段总结"not 'paper or screen?' but 'paper or screen, for this particular task, for this particular reader?'"，即按任务与读者选媒介。
**易错点**：C 用原文出现的"sign-off"做诱饵，但那是举例，不是全部建议。

## 二、仔细阅读 Passage 2（职场 / 心理）

**文章（约 410 词）**

The boundary between work and the rest of life was once drawn by the office door. You arrived, you worked, you left, and the evening belonged to you. Remote work dissolved that door, and with it the clean signal that the working day had ended. For millions of knowledge workers, the laptop now sits on the kitchen table, and the last email of the day is checked just before sleep. Researchers who study burnout call this "boundarylessness"—the loss of the rituals and physical cues that once separated effort from recovery.

Boundarylessness does not simply mean working longer; it means never quite finishing. A survey of remote employees found that a majority reported checking work messages within an hour of waking and again within an hour of going to bed. The cost is not only fatigue. When the brain is never told that work is over, it struggles to shift into the restorative state in which problems are left behind and attention is allowed to wander. Recovery, in other words, is not the absence of work but a distinct activity that requires its own beginning and end.

Some companies have responded by imposing rules of their own: shutting down email servers after a certain hour, or designing software that hides notifications outside office time. These measures help, but they treat the symptom rather than the cause. The deeper issue is that control over the boundary has shifted from the organisation to the individual, and most people are poorly equipped to police themselves. Left to their own devices, they confuse responsiveness with diligence and treat every message as urgent by default.

The most durable solutions are therefore personal rather than institutional. Workers who carve out a fixed "shutdown ritual"—a short walk, a change of clothes, a notebook in which the next day's first task is written down—report better sleep and less Sunday-night dread. What matters is not what the ritual is but that it is a ritual: a repeated, unambiguous signal to the brain that the day is done. In an age when the office door is gone, each of us has to build our own door and, more importantly, learn to close it.

**Q1.** What does the passage identify as the main consequence of remote work?

A. Employees spend more hours working than before.
B. The clear signal that the working day has ended is lost.
C. Companies can no longer monitor their employees.
D. Workers prefer the kitchen table to a proper desk.

**答案 B**　解析：首段点题——远程工作消解了"办公室的门"，随之消失的是"工作日结束的清晰信号"，作者称之为 boundarylessness。B 直接对应。A 是现象之一但非"main consequence"；C、D 未提。
**易错点**：A 有迷惑性，但原文强调的是"边界消失"而非单纯"工时变长"，注意"not simply working longer; never quite finishing"。

**Q2.** According to the passage, recovery is best described as ______.

A. the absence of work
B. a period of deep sleep
C. a distinct activity with its own beginning and end
D. a natural process that happens automatically

**答案 C**　解析：第二段末句"Recovery is not the absence of work but a distinct activity that requires its own beginning and end"。C 为原文。A 被原文否定；B、D 未提或与"需要主动开启/结束"矛盾。
**易错点**：注意 not...but 结构，正确答案在 but 之后。

**Q3.** Why does the author think company-imposed rules are insufficient?

A. They are difficult to enforce among senior staff.
B. They address the symptom rather than the cause.
C. They reduce employees' sense of responsibility.
D. They make employees feel distrusted.

**答案 B**　解析：第三段"These measures help, but they treat the symptom rather than the cause"——公司规则只治标。根本原因是控制权转移到了个人手里。A、C、D 未提。
**易错点**：作者先肯定"these measures help"，容易误以为他支持公司强制，注意 but 转折。

**Q4.** What does the author suggest is the real problem behind the loss of boundary?

A. Individuals are not good at regulating themselves.
B. Technology companies design distracting software.
C. Managers expect employees to be always available.
D. Remote work makes collaboration more difficult.

**答案 A**　解析：第三段"control over the boundary has shifted from the organisation to the individual, and most people are poorly equipped to police themselves"——核心问题是个人不擅自我约束。A 对应。B、C、D 未提。
**易错点**：poorly equipped to police themselves = 不擅自我管理，不是"公司不管理"。

**Q5.** What is the purpose of a "shutdown ritual"?

A. To make employees work more efficiently.
B. To signal to the brain that the day is over.
C. To replace the need for company rules.
D. To shorten the time spent on emails.

**答案 B**　解析：末段"a repeated, unambiguous signal to the brain that the day is done"——关机仪式的作用是给大脑"一天结束"的明确信号。B 正确。A、C、D 未提。
**易错点**：ritual 的具体形式（散步/换衣/写明日任务）不重要，重要的是它的"信号"功能，勿纠结形式选错。

## 三、段落匹配（教育 / 学习方法）

**文章（A–I 九段）**

A. For decades, universities have spoken of teaching students to "think critically," yet rarely defined what this means in practice. The phrase has become so familiar that it is easy to forget how difficult the underlying skill actually is: critical thinking is not a single ability but a bundle of habits—questioning assumptions, weighing evidence, tolerating uncertainty—that develop slowly and unevenly.

B. One obstacle is that students often arrive expecting to be told what to think. Years of schooling that reward correct answers over good questions can leave them ill-prepared for a classroom in which the teacher refuses to offer a final verdict. When the goal shifts from finding the right answer to forming a defensible position, some students experience genuine anxiety.

C. A second obstacle is the structure of assessment. Multiple-choice exams, however efficient, tend to reward recall rather than reasoning. A student can pass such tests without ever having constructed an argument. If the exam is what students study toward, then a curriculum that claims to value critical thinking but measures only memory sends a contradictory message.

D. Some departments have responded by redesigning courses around open-ended problems. In a philosophy seminar, for example, students might be asked to defend two opposing positions on the same issue before arriving at their own. The exercise is uncomfortable by design: it forces students to hold incompatible ideas in mind at once, a capacity psychologists associate with mature reasoning.

E. Writing is another powerful vehicle. A well-designed essay assignment—one that requires students to state a thesis, anticipate objections and revise in light of feedback—is, in effect, a course in reasoning. The trouble is that such assignments demand time, and time is precisely what crowded semesters lack.

F. Class discussion offers a third path, but only when it is managed carefully. Unstructured discussion can easily be dominated by a few confident voices while the rest remain silent. Skilful instructors, by contrast, structure talk so that students must respond to one another's ideas rather than merely perform for the teacher.

G. Technology has introduced new tools, but their record is mixed. Online forums allow quieter students to contribute in writing, which some find liberating. Yet the same platforms can reward speed over reflection, encouraging students to post first and think second.

H. Ultimately, the most reliable predictor of growth is not any single technique but the amount of deliberate practice a student receives. Critical thinking, like any complex skill, improves when it is practised repeatedly, with feedback, in a variety of contexts. No single course, however well designed, can do the job alone.

I. The implication for universities is sobering. If they are serious about producing critical thinkers, they must treat the goal as a whole-curriculum commitment rather than the ambition of a single course, and they must be honest with students—and with themselves—about the time, discomfort and sustained effort the goal requires.

**题目（从 A–I 段中选一段作答）**

**1.** A teaching exercise that forces students to argue both sides of a question before reaching their own conclusion.

**答案 D**　解析：D 段举哲学研讨课为例——"defend two opposing positions on the same issue before arriving at their own"，正是"先论证对立双方再形成己见"的练习。

**2.** The risk that online discussion tools may encourage quick responses rather than careful thought.

**答案 G**　解析：G 段指出技术工具"reward speed over reflection, encouraging students to post first and think second"，即重速度轻思考。

**3.** The observation that students may feel anxious when they are no longer given a single correct answer.

**答案 B**　解析：B 段说明学生习惯被告诉答案，当"the teacher refuses to offer a final verdict"时会产生焦虑。

**4.** A course design that embeds reasoning practice into the writing process.

**答案 E**　解析：E 段指出写作作业——"state a thesis, anticipate objections and revise in light of feedback"——本质上是"一门推理课"。

**5.** The claim that a whole institution, not a single course, must take responsibility for the goal.

**答案 I**　解析：I 段总结——大学若认真培养批判性思维，须"treat the goal as a whole-curriculum commitment rather than the ambition of a single course"。

**6.** A problem with a common form of assessment that rewards memory over reasoning.

**答案 C**　解析：C 段批评选择题"reward recall rather than reasoning"，学生可凭记忆通过却从未构建论证。

**7.** The idea that critical thinking is actually a set of habits that develop gradually.

**答案 A**　解析：A 段定义批判性思维是"a bundle of habits—questioning assumptions, weighing evidence, tolerating uncertainty—that develop slowly and unevenly"。

**8.** The point that classroom discussion needs careful management to avoid being dominated by a few students.

**答案 F**　解析：F 段指出无组织的讨论易被少数人主导，熟练教师会结构化谈话让学生相互回应。

**9.** The assertion that progress depends mostly on repeated practice with feedback.

**答案 H**　解析：H 段强调"the most reliable predictor of growth is the amount of deliberate practice"，即重复练习加反馈。

**10.** The contradiction between claiming to value reasoning while measuring only memory.

**答案 C**　解析：C 段末句——课程声称重视批判性思维却只考记忆，传递了自相矛盾的信息。

## 四、选词填空（睡眠 / 科学）

**文章（15 选 10）**

Sleep has long been dismissed as a passive state, a period in which the brain simply powers down. Neuroscience has since overturned this view. During sleep the brain is (1)____ active, replaying the day's experiences and, crucially, moving information from short-term to long-term storage. This process, known as memory (2)____, is one reason a good night's rest before an exam is more effective than an all-night cram session.

The (3)____ of sleep is not uniform. The night is divided into cycles, each of which includes deep sleep and the rapid-eye-movement stage in which most dreaming occurs. These stages play different roles: deep sleep appears to (4)____ facts and figures, while REM sleep is linked to emotional regulation and creative problem-solving. Losing either stage, therefore, (5)____ different costs.

Modern life, however, is hostile to sleep. Artificial light delays the body's natural (6)____ to grow sleepy, while round-the-clock entertainment provides an endless excuse to stay awake. The result is a growing population that is chronically (7)____-deprived, a condition linked to weakened immunity, impaired judgment and a higher risk of long-term illness.

The good news is that sleep can often be (8)____ with modest changes. Going to bed at a consistent hour, keeping screens out of the bedroom and lowering the temperature slightly are all simple, evidence-backed steps. What matters is not perfection but (9)____: a stable routine, repeated night after night, does more for the brain than an occasional early night ever will. The message from the science is therefore (10)____—treat sleep not as a luxury to be sacrificed, but as a foundation on which every other ability rests.

**选项（A–O，15 选 10）**

A. consolidation　B. rhythm　C. surprisingly　D. stored　E. consistency
F. remarkably　G. architecture　H. restored　I. imposes　J. sleep
K. clear　L. tendency　M. consolidate　N. awake　O. impaired

**答案：**
1. F（remarkably）　2. A（consolidation）　3. G（architecture）　4. M（consolidate）　5. I（imposes）
6. L（tendency）　7. J（sleep）　8. H（restored）　9. E（consistency）　10. K（clear）

**解析：**
1. **F remarkably**：空前是 be 动词，空后是形容词 active，需副词修饰；"睡眠中大脑异常活跃"，remarkably（显著地）最合语境，surprisingly 语义可但强调"惊讶"，不如 remarkably 客观。
2. **A consolidation**：空格前是 memory（名词作定语），需名词；"记忆巩固"是固定搭配 memory consolidation。
3. **G architecture**：the + 名词 + of，且后文讲"睡眠分期/结构"，sleep architecture（睡眠结构）是术语。
4. **M consolidate**：appears to 后接动词原形；"深睡眠巩固事实性记忆"，与第 2 题 consolidation 呼应。
5. **I imposes**：空后是 different costs（宾语），需及物动词第三人称单数；"带来不同代价"，impose costs 搭配。
6. **L tendency**：natural + 名词；"身体自然产生睡意的倾向"，tendency。
7. **J sleep**：空后是 deprived（过去分词），构成复合形容词 sleep-deprived（睡眠不足的），固定搭配。
8. **H restored**：be + 过去分词；"睡眠可以通过适度调整得以恢复"，restored。
9. **E consistency**：but 后是 not perfection but ____，需与 perfection 并列的名词；"重要的是持之以恒"，consistency。
10. **K clear**：is + 形容词作表语；"科学传递的信息是明确的"，clear。

**易错点**：第 1 题 remarkably / surprisingly 易混——surprisingly 暗含"出乎意料"，而文中是陈述"睡眠中大脑其实非常活跃"，用 remarkably 更中性准确；第 9 题勿误选 rhythm（节奏），but 结构要求与 perfection 对立的"稳定性/坚持"，consistency 更贴切。

## 五、写作（Directions 标准六级要求）

**题目：** For this part, you are allowed 30 minutes to write an essay on the importance of learning to say no. You should write at least 150 words but no more than 200 words.

**参考范文（约 180 词）：**

The ability to say no is often mistaken for rudeness, yet it is in fact one of the most valuable skills a person can develop. Learning to decline is not about rejecting others; it is about protecting the time and energy that every meaningful commitment requires.

The first reason to practise saying no is that attention is a limited resource. Every request we accept consumes hours we cannot recover, and a person who agrees to everything quickly finds that the things he genuinely cares about are left unfinished. Saying no is therefore a way of saying yes to what matters. A second reason is that honest refusal preserves relationships. A polite, prompt no is far kinder than a reluctant yes followed by broken promises and quiet resentment.

Of course, saying no requires courage. It is easier to nod and regret it later than to decline and face a moment of awkwardness. But the long-term cost of a life lived to please others is far greater than the short-term discomfort of a firm but courteous refusal. In the end, learning to say no is not a retreat from the world; it is the foundation of a life lived with intention.

**评分要点（供自评）：**
- 立场明确（首段即亮出"say no 不是拒绝别人，是保护资源"）
- 论证有层次：有限注意力 → 维护关系，两条理由递进
- 让步反驳段（Of course... But...）提升思辨深度
- 衔接自然：first / a second / of course / in the end
- 语言准确、句式有变化，无中式表达

**易失分点**：只喊口号不给机制；通篇简单句；把 say no 写成"冷漠"，偏离"有原则的拒绝"这一立意。

## 六、翻译（汉译英）

**原文：** 中国的高铁网络是世界上最繁忙的铁路系统之一。它把许多中小城市与大都市连接起来，使人们可以在一天之内往返于相距千里的两地。高铁不仅缩短了出行时间，也改变了人们选择工作和居住地的方式。近年来，中国还向海外输出高铁技术，帮助多个国家建设现代化铁路，让高铁成为中国制造的一张亮丽名片。

**参考译文：**

China's high-speed rail network is one of the busiest railway systems in the world. It connects many small and medium-sized cities with major metropolises, enabling people to travel between places a thousand kilometers apart and return within a single day. High-speed rail has not only shortened travel time but also changed the way people choose where to work and live. In recent years, China has also exported high-speed rail technology overseas, helping several countries build modern railways, which has made high-speed rail a shining symbol of Chinese manufacturing.

**采分点：**
- "最繁忙的之一" → one of the busiest ... in the world（最高级 + of 结构）
- "使人们可以……往返" → enabling people to ... and return（分词作结果状语）
- "不仅……也……" → not only ... but also ...（not only 置句首才倒装，此处不倒装）
- "相距千里" → a thousand kilometers apart（勿直译 thousand miles）
- "亮丽名片" → a shining symbol / a calling card（意译，勿逐字）

**易错点**：时态——前半描述现状用一般现在时，末句"近年来……还输出"用现在完成时（has exported）；"使人们可以"用分词 enabling 比 so that 更简洁地道。
