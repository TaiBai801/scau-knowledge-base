## 一、仔细阅读 Passage 1（心理 / 自控）

**文章（约 410 词）**

Procrastination is usually described as a failure of time management, a problem to be solved with better schedules and stricter deadlines. But psychologists who study it have reached a different conclusion: the real problem is not that procrastinators cannot manage time, but that they cannot manage emotion. The task they avoid is rarely too hard; it is too uncomfortable—boring, difficult, or freighted with the fear of doing it badly.

From this perspective, procrastination is a form of mood repair. Faced with a task that triggers anxiety or self-doubt, the procrastinator does what any sensible creature would do and moves toward something pleasant instead. The relief is real but short-lived. The task has not disappeared; it has only grown larger in the mind, and the guilt of avoidance now joins the original discomfort.

This explains why self-criticism makes things worse. The standard advice—be harder on yourself—rests on the assumption that the problem is laziness, which can be scolded away. But shame tends to deepen the very negative emotions that drive avoidance in the first place. People who respond to procrastination with self-blame are more likely to procrastinate again, not less.

The more effective strategies work by lowering the emotional temperature rather than raising the stakes. One is to shrink the task: not "write the report" but "write the first paragraph." Another is to make the work more bearable by pairing it with something pleasant, or by forgiving oneself for past delay—a step that sounds indulgent but has been shown, in studies, to reduce future procrastination.

The deepest change, however, comes from a shift in what the task means. When a piece of work is seen as a test of one's worth, every setback feels like a verdict. When it is seen simply as something to be done, a little at a time, the fear that feeds procrastination loses much of its power. The enemy, it turns out, is not the work. It is the meaning we attach to it.

**Q1.** According to psychologists, what is the real cause of procrastination?

A. Poor time management skills.
B. An inability to manage emotion.
C. A lack of self-discipline.
D. Overly strict deadlines.

**答案 B**　解析：首段"the real problem is not that procrastinators cannot manage time, but that they cannot manage emotion"。B 对应。
**易错点**：not...but... 结构，答案在 but 之后；A 是作者明确否定的旧观点。

**Q2.** What does the author mean by calling procrastination "a form of mood repair"?

A. It is a way to temporarily relieve discomfort.
B. It is a strategy for getting more work done.
C. It is a sign of a serious mental illness.
D. It is a habit that improves with practice.

**答案 A**　解析：第二段"the procrastinator... moves toward something pleasant instead. The relief is real but short-lived"——拖延是一种短暂缓解不适的方式。A 对应。
**易错点**：mood repair 是"情绪修复"，指逃避带来的短暂轻松，注意"short-lived"。

**Q3.** Why does self-criticism make procrastination worse?

A. It wastes time that could be spent working.
B. It deepens the negative emotions that cause avoidance.
C. It makes people lazy and dependent.
D. It conflicts with the advice of experts.

**答案 B**　解析：第三段"shame tends to deepen the very negative emotions that drive avoidance in the first place"。B 对应。
**易错点**：shame 加深的是"导致逃避的负面情绪"，形成恶性循环，勿选 A。

**Q4.** Which strategy does the author mention as effective?

A. Setting stricter deadlines.
B. Being harder on oneself.
C. Shrinking the task into a smaller first step.
D. Avoiding pleasant activities during work.

**答案 C**　解析：第四段"not 'write the report' but 'write the first paragraph'"——把任务缩小到第一步。C 对应。
**易错点**：有效策略是"降温"而非"加压"，A、B 是作者否定的做法。

**Q5.** What is the deepest change the author recommends?

A. Changing what the task means to oneself.
B. Working longer hours each day.
C. Ignoring negative emotions entirely.
D. Seeking professional help.

**答案 A**　解析：末段"a shift in what the task means"——把工作从"对自身价值的检验"重新理解为"一点点去做的事"。A 对应。
**易错点**：the meaning we attach to it 是关键，指"我们赋予任务的意义"，不是"延长工时"。

## 二、仔细阅读 Passage 2（社会 / 社区）

**文章（约 400 词）**

In the middle of the last century, the sociologist Ray Oldenburg coined a phrase that has since entered the language of urban planning: the "third place." After home and the workplace, he argued, people need a third kind of setting—a café, a barbershop, a park bench—where they can gather informally, on neutral ground, without an appointment or an agenda. These places, Oldenburg believed, are not luxuries but load-bearing walls of community.

Half a century later, the third place is in retreat. Rising rents have pushed the independent café and the corner store out of many neighbourhoods. Long commutes and longer working hours have shrunk the time people have for casual socialising. And the smartphone, which connects us to distant friends, has simultaneously weakened the habit of talking to the person in the next chair. The result is a quiet epidemic of loneliness that public health researchers now describe in terms once reserved for smoking and obesity.

The loss is not only emotional. Third places perform quiet economic and civic functions. They are where reputations are made, where job leads are exchanged, where newcomers learn the unwritten rules of a place. A street without them may look fine in photographs, but it lacks the connective tissue that turns a collection of houses into a neighbourhood.

Rebuilding them is difficult because they cannot simply be designed into existence. Planners can provide squares and benches, but a place becomes a third place only through the steady, unscripted use of the people who come. That is why the most promising efforts are often small: extending library hours, allowing street vendors to trade, protecting the cheap seats in a café where people are not rushed to leave.

What these measures share is a recognition that community is not a product to be delivered but a practice to be sustained. The third place is valuable not because it is architecturally fine, but because it is open, cheap and unhurried—qualities that modern life, for all its efficiency, has found surprisingly hard to preserve.

**Q1.** What is a "third place" according to Ray Oldenburg?

A. A place where people gather informally outside home and work.
B. A workspace that is neither home nor office.
C. A luxury restaurant for special occasions.
D. A public building designed by planners.

**答案 A**　解析：首段"a third kind of setting—a café, a barbershop, a park bench—where they can gather informally, on neutral ground"。A 对应。
**易错点**：third place 是"家与工作之外的第三类场所"，不是"工作场所"（B）。

**Q2.** Which factor is mentioned as weakening the third place?

A. The decline of public parks.
B. The smartphone, which reduces face-to-face talk.
C. The rise of luxury housing.
D. The growth of online shopping.

**答案 B**　解析：第二段"the smartphone... has simultaneously weakened the habit of talking to the person in the next chair"。B 对应。
**易错点**：手机"连接远方却削弱身边交谈"，这是并列的三因素之一，勿选文中未提的 D。

**Q3.** The author compares loneliness to ______.

A. a passing social trend
B. smoking and obesity
C. a mental illness
D. an urban myth

**答案 B**　解析：第二段末"a quiet epidemic of loneliness that public health researchers now describe in terms once reserved for smoking and obesity"。B 对应。
**易错点**：in terms once reserved for = "过去只用于（形容）……"，是类比，不是"等同于精神疾病"。

**Q4.** What quiet function do third places perform?

A. They generate tax revenue for the city.
B. They serve as venues for formal events.
C. They are where reputations are made and leads exchanged.
D. They provide housing for newcomers.

**答案 C**　解析：第三段"where reputations are made, where job leads are exchanged, where newcomers learn the unwritten rules"。C 对应。
**易错点**：quiet economic and civic functions 指"声誉、人脉、潜规则"这些隐性功能，不是税收或住房。

**Q5.** Why does the author say third places "cannot simply be designed into existence"?

A. They require the steady, unscripted use of people.
B. They are too expensive for planners to build.
C. They depend on favourable weather conditions.
D. They conflict with modern architecture.

**答案 A**　解析：第四段"a place becomes a third place only through the steady, unscripted use of the people who come"。A 对应。
**易错点**：design 与 use 的对立——规划者能提供场地，但"第三场所"要靠人的自发使用才成立。

## 三、段落匹配（大学教育 / 国际化）

**文章（A–I 九段）**

A. Internationalisation has become one of the most repeated words in higher education. Universities compete to recruit foreign students, send their own abroad, and sign partnerships across borders. Yet beneath the confident talk lies an unresolved question: what, exactly, is internationalisation for?

B. For some, the answer is economic. Foreign students pay higher fees, and their spending supports local economies. This logic is powerful, but it has a cost: when students are treated primarily as a source of revenue, the educational relationship is easily distorted.

C. For others, the goal is cultural. Exposure to different backgrounds, it is argued, broadens students' minds and prepares them for a globalised world. This is the most commonly cited benefit, though it is also the hardest to measure.

D. A third view emphasises the academic: international collaboration raises the quality of research by bringing together people who would otherwise never work together. Here the unit of benefit is less the individual student than the institution and the field.

E. In practice, these goals frequently collide. A university that recruits students for revenue may do little to integrate them; a partnership signed for prestige may produce no real collaboration. The label "international" can be attached to almost anything, which is precisely the problem.

F. Measurement has not helped. Rankings reward the number of foreign students and the share of international staff, encouraging universities to chase these figures rather than ask what the numbers achieve. What gets counted, in short, is not always what counts.

G. Some institutions have begun to resist this drift. They have scaled back recruitment targets and focused instead on the quality of the experience—on making sure that foreign students are genuinely integrated, and that partnerships lead to shared teaching and research.

H. A common theme in these reforms is reciprocity. Internationalisation works best when it is a two-way exchange in which both sides learn, rather than a one-way flow of students and money. Reciprocity is difficult to build and easy to fake, which may be why it is so often missing.

I. The future of internationalisation, then, depends less on how many students cross borders than on what happens after they arrive—on whether universities treat international students as members of a community rather than customers of a business, and on whether partnerships are judged by their substance rather than their signature.

**题目（从 A–I 段中选一段作答）**

**1.** The problem that "international" can be attached to almost anything.

**答案 E**　解析：E 段指出各目标在实践中冲突，"the label 'international' can be attached to almost anything, which is precisely the problem"。

**2.** The view that international collaboration improves research quality.

**答案 D**　解析：D 段"the academic"观点——国际合作通过汇聚人才提升研究质量。

**3.** The economic motive of recruiting foreign students for fees and spending.

**答案 B**　解析：B 段"the economic"观点——外国学生付更高学费、消费支持本地经济。

**4.** The tendency of rankings to reward headcounts rather than substance.

**答案 F**　解析：F 段指出排名奖励学生人数和海外教师占比，让大学追逐数字而非成果。

**5.** The idea that reciprocity is a two-way exchange that is hard to build.

**答案 H**　解析：H 段强调 reciprocity（互惠）是双向交流，难建也易伪装。

**6.** The claim that the cultural benefit is the most cited but hardest to measure.

**答案 C**　解析：C 段"the cultural"观点——跨文化接触开阔视野，最常被提及却最难衡量。

**7.** The argument that the future depends on what happens after students arrive.

**答案 I**　解析：I 段总结——未来取决于学生抵达之后，大学是否视其为社区成员而非客户。

**8.** The unresolved question of what internationalisation is for.

**答案 A**　解析：A 段点出核心未决问题——国际化到底是为了什么。

**9.** Evidence that some institutions now focus on quality over recruitment targets.

**答案 G**　解析：G 段指出一些大学缩减招生目标、转而重视体验质量与真正融合。

**10.** The danger of treating students mainly as a source of revenue.

**答案 B**　解析：B 段警告"当学生主要被当作收入来源，教育关系就被扭曲了"。

## 四、选词填空（城市规划 / 步行城市）

**文章（15 选 10）**

For most of the past century, the car was treated as the default citizen of the city. Streets were widened for it, land was set aside for it, and the comfort of the driver routinely (1)____ the safety of everyone else. A growing movement now proposes to reverse that priority, arguing that cities should be designed first for the pedestrian, and that doing so is not a nostalgic gesture but a (2)____ of public health, economic vitality and environmental sense.

The health case is the most straightforward. Walking is one of the cheapest and most effective forms of exercise, yet in many cities the built environment actively (3)____ it—sidewalks that end abruptly, crossings timed for cars, neighbourhoods where daily needs are too far to reach on foot. People are not lazy by nature; they simply (4)____ to the cues their surroundings give them.

The economic case is less obvious but equally strong. Studies of shopping streets find that pedestrians spend more, over time, than drivers, and that foot traffic supports a density of small businesses that car-dependent strips cannot (5)____. A street that is pleasant to walk along is, almost by definition, a street where people linger and spend.

There is, of course, resistance. Businesses fear that removing parking will (6)____ customers, and commuters fear losing the speed they value. But the evidence from cities that have tried suggests these fears are often (7)____: when streets are made safer and more pleasant, people do not vanish—they arrive in greater numbers, on foot.

The transition is not easy, and it cannot happen overnight. It requires (8)____ change—redesigning intersections, reallocating road space, investing in public transport—and the political will to accept short-term disruption. Yet the alternative is to keep building cities for machines rather than for people, a choice whose costs become clearer with every (9)____ summer and every report on the health harms of sedentary life. The question is not whether cities will change, but whether they will change with (10)____, or only after the costs of inaction have become impossible to ignore.

**选项（A–O，15 选 10）**

A. discourage　B. driving　C. exaggerated　D. hotter　E. intention
F. lose　G. matter　H. outweigh　I. patchwork　J. patience
K. reinvention　L. respond　M. sustain　N. verdict　O. worsen

**答案：**
1. H（outweigh）　2. G（matter）　3. A（discourage）　4. L（respond）　5. M（sustain）
6. F（lose）　7. C（exaggerated）　8. K（reinvention）　9. D（hotter）　10. J（patience）

**解析：**
1. **H outweigh**：司机舒适 routinely ____ 他人的安全；"常常凌驾于他人安全之上"，outweigh（超过/重于）。
2. **G matter**：a ____ of public health；"这是关乎公共健康、经济活力与环境意识的事"，a matter of（关乎……的事）。
3. **A discourage**：built environment actively ____ walking；"建成环境主动阻碍步行"，discourage（使气馁/阻碍）。
4. **L respond**：people ____ to the cues；"人对环境线索作出反应"，respond to。
5. **M sustain**：foot traffic supports a density of small businesses that car-dependent strips cannot ____；"步行人流支撑的商铺密度是依赖汽车的商业街无法维持的"，sustain（维持）。
6. **F lose**：removing parking will ____ customers；"移除停车位会流失顾客"，lose。
7. **C exaggerated**：these fears are often ____；"这些担忧往往被夸大"，exaggerated。
8. **K reinvention**：requires ____ change；"需要重塑式的变革"，reinvention（再造）。
9. **D hotter**：every ____ summer；"每个更炎热的夏天"，hotter（比较级，呼应气候变暖）。
10. **J patience**：change with ____；"以耐心推进变革"，patience。

**易错点**：第 5 题 sustain 与第 6 题 lose 语义相反，注意上下文方向；第 9 题 hotter 是"越来越热"的比较级，呼应后文气候与久坐健康危害。

## 五、写作（Directions 标准六级要求）

**题目：** For this part, you are allowed 30 minutes to write an essay on the importance of volunteering. You should write at least 150 words but no more than 200 words.

**参考范文（约 180 词）：**

Volunteering is often framed as an act of generosity—something given freely to others. What this framing overlooks is how much the volunteer receives in return. The value of volunteering, properly understood, runs in both directions.

For the community, volunteers supply labour and care that would otherwise be unaffordable: they staff food banks, tutor struggling students and keep local events alive. For the volunteer, the benefits are quieter but no less real. Volunteering builds skills that classrooms rarely teach—organising, communicating, solving problems under pressure—and it offers a sense of purpose that paid work does not always provide. Many volunteers report that giving their time is among the most reliable sources of meaning in their lives.

This is not to suggest that volunteering should be approached as a transaction, with an eye on what it can return. The moment it becomes purely self-interested, it loses something essential. But there is no contradiction in saying that helping others and helping oneself are, in this case, the same act. A society in which people freely give their time is not only kinder but also stronger, because the bonds it creates are exactly the ones no salary can buy.

**评分要点（供自评）：**
- 破题：指出"志愿=单向付出"的常见误读，点出双向价值
- 分两层：对社区（物质/照护）+ 对志愿者（技能/意义），由外而内
- 转折澄清"不是交易"，守住立意，避免功利化
- 结尾升华：互助即自助，社会因此更紧密

**易失分点**：把 volunteering 写成"好人好事"流水账；只写付出不写收获；或走向另一极端把志愿写成纯粹利己。

## 六、翻译（汉译英）

**原文：** 长城是中国古代劳动人民智慧的结晶，也是世界建筑史上的一项伟大奇迹。它绵延上万里，跨越崇山峻岭，历经两千多年的风雨依然巍然屹立。如今，长城早已不再是抵御外敌的军事屏障，而是中华民族坚韧不屈精神的象征，每年吸引着数以百万计的中外游客前来参观。

**参考译文：**

The Great Wall is a crystallization of the wisdom of the ancient Chinese working people and a great wonder in the history of world architecture. Stretching over ten thousand li and crossing countless mountains, it has stood firm through more than two thousand years of wind and rain. Today, the Great Wall is no longer a military barrier against foreign invaders but a symbol of the perseverance and unyielding spirit of the Chinese nation, attracting millions of tourists from home and abroad every year.

**采分点：**
- "智慧的结晶" → a crystallization of the wisdom（固定表达）
- "绵延上万里，跨越崇山峻岭" → Stretching over ten thousand li and crossing countless mountains（分词并列）
- "历经……依然巍然屹立" → has stood firm through ...（现在完成时）
- "不再是……而是……" → is no longer ... but ...
- "数以百万计的中外游客" → millions of tourists from home and abroad

**易错点**：主体时态——前半描述客观事实与现状用一般现在时，"历经两千多年依然屹立"用现在完成时（has stood）；"里"保留音译 li 并可用 ten thousand li，勿硬译成 mile。
