## 一、仔细阅读 Passage 1（健康 / 运动科学）

**文章（约 410 词）**

For decades, the message from public health was reassuringly simple: sitting too much is bad for you, so stand up and move. Offices responded with standing desks, and wearable devices began to nag their owners to hit ten thousand steps. But as the evidence has matured, a more awkward finding has emerged: the harm of sitting may have less to do with the sitting itself than with what it replaces, and the fix is not nearly as simple as swapping a chair for a stool.

The original studies compared people who sat for long stretches with those who moved often, and the movers came out ahead on almost every measure. From this, researchers concluded that prolonged sitting caused the damage. But sitting and physical inactivity are not the same thing, and later work that separated the two found that total movement matters far more than the number of hours spent in a chair. A person who sits eight hours a day but also runs regularly is in far less trouble than one who sits six hours and never exercises at all.

The standing desk, meanwhile, has turned out to be a weak remedy. Standing burns only marginally more energy than sitting, and long periods of standing bring their own problems—leg fatigue, back strain, and for some, an increased risk of circulation problems. The honest conclusion is that posture alone, whether seated or upright, is a poor substitute for actual movement.

What the body appears to need is not a single position but variety: frequent, brief interruptions to long periods of stillness. The most protective habits are unglamorous—taking the stairs, walking to a colleague's desk instead of emailing, standing up during phone calls. None of these requires equipment, and none needs to be done for long. Their value lies in breaking up the unbroken hours, not in counting steps or burning calories.

The lesson, then, is a rebuke to the idea that health can be bought with a clever gadget. The body was not designed to be still, but neither was it designed for a single corrective posture. Movement, in small and ordinary doses, spread through the whole day, remains the one intervention the evidence truly supports.

**Q1.** What does the passage say about the early studies on sitting?

A. They proved that standing desks are effective.
B. They confused sitting with physical inactivity.
C. They focused too much on exercise intensity.
D. They were later found to be completely wrong.

**答案 B**　解析：第二段"Sitting and physical inactivity are not the same thing, and later work that separated the two found that total movement matters far more"——早期研究把"久坐"和"缺乏运动"混为一谈。B 对应。
**易错点**：早期研究不是"全错"，而是"没区分两个变量"，勿选 D（completely wrong）。

**Q2.** According to the passage, who is in greater trouble?

A. A person who sits eight hours and never exercises.
B. A person who sits six hours and never exercises.
C. A person who sits eight hours but exercises regularly.
D. A person who uses a standing desk all day.

**答案 B**　解析：第二段末"one who sits six hours and never exercises at all"比"坐 8 小时但常跑步"更糟。B 对应。
**易错点**：陷阱在于"坐得久≠最糟"，关键变量是"是否运动"。

**Q3.** What is the author's view of standing desks?

A. They are an excellent solution to prolonged sitting.
B. They are a weak remedy with problems of their own.
C. They should replace all office chairs.
D. They work only for people with back pain.

**答案 B**　解析：第三段"the standing desk has turned out to be a weak remedy"，且"long periods of standing bring their own problems"。B 对应。
**易错点**：作者对站立桌是"弱效 + 有副作用"，既非全盘肯定也非全盘否定。

**Q4.** What kind of habit does the author consider most protective?

A. Long, intense workouts in the gym.
B. Frequent, brief interruptions to stillness.
C. Maintaining perfect posture while seated.
D. Reaching ten thousand steps every day.

**答案 B**　解析：第四段"frequent, brief interruptions to long periods of stillness"是最护身的习惯。B 对应。A、C、D 都被作者否定或弱化。
**易错点**：作者明确否定"计步数/算卡路里"（their value lies in breaking up the unbroken hours, not in counting steps）。

**Q5.** What is the main lesson of the passage?

A. Health can be bought with clever gadgets.
B. Movement should be spread through the day in small doses.
C. Standing is always healthier than sitting.
D. The body needs one correct posture.

**答案 B**　解析：末段"Movement, in small and ordinary doses, spread through the whole day, remains the one intervention the evidence truly supports"。B 对应。
**易错点**：作者否定"单一姿势"和"买设备换健康"，落点是"全天分散的小剂量活动"。

## 二、仔细阅读 Passage 2（经济 / 零工经济）

**文章（约 400 词）**

The rise of app-based gig work—driving, delivering, running errands on demand—was once celebrated as the future of work: flexible, low-barrier and available to anyone with a phone. A decade on, that optimism has cooled. What looked like freedom to its advocates now looks, to a growing number of critics, like a transfer of risk from companies to the people who do the work.

The core of the criticism is classification. Gig platforms typically treat workers not as employees but as independent contractors, which relieves the companies of obligations such as minimum wage, sick pay and insurance. Workers gain the right to choose their own hours, but they absorb the costs that a stable employer would once have carried: a slow week, an illness, a broken vehicle. The flexibility, in other words, has a price, and it is paid almost entirely by one side.

This is not to deny the genuine appeal of the model. For students, parents and others who need to fit work around life, the ability to decide when to work is valuable, and no amount of criticism erases that. Nor is every platform the same: some now offer benefits, and a few jurisdictions have legislated to reclassify drivers as workers with rights.

The difficulty is that regulation has struggled to keep pace. Labour law was built around the image of a stable, full-time job, and it fits the new arrangements poorly. Questions that were once clear—who is an employee, what is a workplace—have become genuinely hard to answer, and different countries have answered them in incompatible ways.

What is emerging, slowly, is a search for a middle ground: a category of worker who is neither fully independent nor fully employed, entitled to some protections but not all. Designing such a category is technically demanding, and it will not please everyone. But the alternative—allowing the boundary to be settled case by case, court by court—is likely to produce exactly the uncertainty that both workers and platforms say they want to avoid.

**Q1.** What has happened to the optimism about gig work over the past decade?

A. It has been completely replaced by criticism.
B. It has cooled as critics highlight risk transfer.
C. It has strengthened as platforms added benefits.
D. It has remained unchanged.

**答案 B**　解析：首段末"that optimism has cooled... a transfer of risk from companies to the people who do the work"。B 对应。A 的"completely"过度；C 与"cooled"相反。
**易错点**：作者是"降温"不是"全盘否定"，勿选 A。

**Q2.** What is at the core of the criticism of gig platforms?

A. The low quality of their services.
B. The classification of workers as contractors.
C. The high fees charged to customers.
D. The lack of a stable internet connection.

**答案 B**　解析：第二段"The core of the criticism is classification"——核心是"把工人当独立承包方而非雇员"。B 对应。
**易错点**：classification 是关键词，指劳动关系的定性，不是服务质量或收费。

**Q3.** What do workers actually pay for the flexibility they gain?

A. The cost of a stable employer's obligations.
B. The price of their smartphones.
C. The fees charged by the platform.
D. The time spent learning new skills.

**答案 A**　解析：第二段"they absorb the costs that a stable employer would once have carried"——工人自己承担了原本由雇主承担的成本。A 对应。
**易错点**：flexibility has a price，且"paid almost entirely by one side"，注意是工人这方承担。

**Q4.** Why has regulation struggled with gig work?

A. Labour law was built around stable, full-time jobs.
B. Governments are unwilling to regulate platforms.
C. Platforms refuse to share data with regulators.
D. Workers disagree about what rights they want.

**答案 A**　解析：第四段"Labour law was built around the image of a stable, full-time job, and it fits the new arrangements poorly"。A 对应。
**易错点**：regulation 的困境源于法律框架过时，不是政府"不愿"或平台"拒交数据"。

**Q5.** What solution is emerging according to the passage?

A. Reclassifying all gig workers as full employees.
B. Eliminating gig work platforms altogether.
C. Creating a middle category of worker with partial protections.
D. Leaving each case to be settled by the courts.

**答案 C**　解析：末段"a category of worker who is neither fully independent nor fully employed, entitled to some protections but not all"。C 对应。D 是作者警告要避免的。
**易错点**：middle ground = 中间地带，勿选两个极端（A、B），D 是作者反对的替代方案。

## 三、段落匹配（文化 / 博物馆）

**文章（A–I 九段）**

A. Museums have spent the past generation trying to shake off their reputation as dusty warehouses. The modern museum wants to be lively, interactive and, above all, relevant to people who might never have set foot in one. Behind this ambition lies a real tension: how far can an institution change to attract visitors before it stops doing what only a museum can do?

B. One pressure is financial. Public funding has declined in many countries, and museums have been pushed to earn more of their own income. This encourages blockbuster exhibitions, retail and catering—activities that bring crowds and cash but can overshadow the quieter work of scholarship and conservation.

C. A second pressure is technological. Virtual tours, digital collections and social media have made objects accessible to far more people than any physical building could hold. Yet there is a difference between seeing an image of a painting and standing before it, and museums are still working out how to value the latter.

D. Some institutions have embraced participation wholeheartedly, inviting visitors to co-create exhibitions or vote on what to display. The results can be energising, but they also raise questions about expertise: if everyone is a curator, who decides what is worth preserving?

E. Others have gone the opposite way, doubling down on scholarship and accepting smaller audiences. These museums argue that their job is not to please crowds but to safeguard objects and the knowledge attached to them, even when that work attracts little attention.

F. Between these poles, a consensus has begun to form around the idea of the museum as a "third place"—neither home nor workplace, but a public space where people gather, linger and encounter ideas at their own pace. This vision values both community and contemplation.

G. Education has moved to the centre of this vision. Museums now design programmes for schools, run talks and workshops, and measure success partly by what visitors learn rather than merely by how many pass through the turnstiles.

H. The challenge is that education, done well, is expensive and hard to measure, while attendance is cheap to count. Institutions under budget pressure may be tempted to chase the easy number and let the deeper purpose slide.

I. The museums that navigate this tension successfully are not those that choose between popularity and scholarship, but those that treat the two as reinforcing. A lively museum that is also rigorous does not sacrifice depth for crowds; it uses the crowds to fund and justify the depth.

**题目（从 A–I 段中选一段作答）**

**1.** A vision of the museum as a public space between home and work.

**答案 F**　解析：F 段提出"third place"——既非家也非职场，而是人们聚集、停留、按自己节奏接触思想的公共空间。

**2.** The financial pressure to earn income, which can overshadow scholarship.

**答案 B**　解析：B 段指出公共拨款下降，博物馆被迫靠特展、零售、餐饮创收，却可能盖过学术与保管工作。

**3.** The view that a museum's job is to safeguard objects even with small audiences.

**答案 E**　解析：E 段描述"反方向"的博物馆——坚守学术、接受小规模观众，使命是守护藏品与知识。

**4.** The difference between viewing a digital image and standing before the original.

**答案 C**　解析：C 段指出虚拟导览让更多人可及，但"看画的图片"与"站在原作前"仍有差别。

**5.** The temptation to chase easily counted attendance over deeper purpose.

**答案 H**　解析：H 段指出教育难做且难衡量、人流易数，预算压力下博物馆可能追逐易得数字而丢掉深层使命。

**6.** The question of who decides what is worth preserving when everyone participates.

**答案 D**　解析：D 段在"邀请观众共创"后提出"if everyone is a curator, who decides what is worth preserving?"。

**7.** The claim that successful museums treat popularity and scholarship as reinforcing.

**答案 I**　解析：I 段总结——成功的博物馆不二选一，而是让热闹与严谨互相成就。

**8.** The growing importance of education in museums.

**答案 G**　解析：G 段指出教育移到核心，博物馆办课程、讲座、工作坊，以"学到什么"衡量成功。

**9.** The fundamental tension behind the modern museum's ambitions.

**答案 A**　解析：A 段提出根本矛盾——博物馆能为吸引观众改变到什么程度，才不至于丧失"只有博物馆能做"的职能。

**10.** The pressure from technology to make collections digitally accessible.

**答案 C**　解析：C 段开头"technological pressure"，虚拟导览、数字馆藏、社交媒体的可及性压力。

## 四、选词填空（数字隐私 / 网络素养）

**文章（15 选 10）**

The internet promised a world of free information, but its real currency has turned out to be something else: attention, and the personal data that directs it. Every click, search and pause is recorded, and from these traces companies build (1)____ profiles that determine what we see, what we pay and sometimes how we are judged. The result is a system that is highly convenient and quietly (2)____.

Most users are vaguely aware of this, yet few act on that awareness. Part of the reason is that the cost is invisible: surrendering data feels (3)____ because nothing is visibly taken away. Part is that the alternatives are inconvenient—reading privacy policies is tedious, and disabling tracking often breaks the very services people rely on.

A more fundamental problem is asymmetry of knowledge. Companies know a great deal about users, while users know almost nothing about how their data is used. This (4)____ makes informed consent difficult, since one cannot meaningfully agree to terms one does not understand.

Policymakers have begun to respond. Regulations now require companies to obtain explicit consent and to explain, in plain language, what data they collect and why. The aim is not to end data collection—much of it is genuinely useful—but to (5)____ the balance of power, giving individuals a real say in the bargain.

Technology alone will not solve the problem, but it can help. Privacy tools are becoming easier to use, and some platforms now let users (6)____ exactly what is shared with advertisers. The harder task is cultural: rebuilding the (7)____ that privacy is a right worth defending, not a luxury to trade away.

The choice, in the end, is not between a life online and a life off the grid. It is between two ways of being online—one in which individuals remain the (8)____ of their own data, and one in which they are merely its source. The difference is small on the surface and (9)____ beneath it, and it will be settled not by any single law or tool, but by millions of ordinary (10)____ made every day.

**选项（A–O，15 选 10）**

A. detailed　B. shift　C. painless　D. masters　E. imbalance
F. decisions　G. restore　H. belief　I. decide　J. enormous
K. invasive　L. control　M. expensive　N. powerless　O. inspect

**答案：**
1. A（detailed）　2. K（invasive）　3. C（painless）　4. E（imbalance）　5. B（shift）
6. L（control）　7. H（belief）　8. D（masters）　9. J（enormous）　10. F（decisions）

**解析：**
1. **A detailed**：build + 形容词 + profiles；"构建详尽的用户画像"，detailed profiles。
2. **K invasive**：quietly + 形容词；"这个系统高度便利却又悄悄侵入隐私"，invasive（侵入性的）。
3. **C painless**：surrender data feels ____；"交出数据感觉无痛，因为没什么被可见地拿走"，painless。
4. **E imbalance**：this ____ makes informed consent difficult；"知识的不对称/失衡使知情同意困难"，imbalance。
5. **B shift**：to ____ the balance of power；"改变权力平衡"，shift the balance。
6. **L control**：let users ____ what is shared；"让用户控制分享什么"，control。
7. **H belief**：rebuilding the ____ that privacy is a right；"重建'隐私是值得捍卫的权利'这一信念"，belief（后接 that 同位语从句）。
8. **D masters**：individuals remain the ____ of their own data；"个人仍是自己数据的主人"，masters。
9. **J enormous**：small on the surface and ____ beneath；"表面微小、深层巨大"，enormous 与 small 对举。
10. **F decisions**：millions of ordinary ____；"数百万个日常决定"，decisions。

**易错点**：第 7 题 belief 后接 that 同位语从句是判断依据，勿选 control（无法接从句）；第 9 题 small 的反义是 enormous，勿选 expensive。

## 五、写作（Directions 标准六级要求）

**题目：** For this part, you are allowed 30 minutes to write an essay on the impact of mobile payment on people's daily life. You should write at least 150 words but no more than 200 words.

**参考范文（约 180 词）：**

A few years ago, paying for almost anything in China meant carrying cash or a wallet full of cards. Today, a phone is all that is required. Mobile payment has changed daily life so thoroughly that many young people no longer remember the world that preceded it.

The most obvious change is convenience. Transactions that once required counting change now take seconds, and the same applies to everything from bus fares to utility bills. Mobile payment has also made money more transparent: every payment leaves a record, which helps people track their spending and manage their finances more deliberately. For small businesses, the technology has lowered barriers—a street vendor needs nothing more than a printed code to accept payment.

Yet the change is not without cost. The ease of paying can encourage careless spending, and those without smartphones, particularly the elderly, risk being left behind as services move online. These concerns are a reminder that convenience should not come at the price of inclusion. The challenge ahead is not to reverse the technology but to make sure its benefits are shared by everyone.

**评分要点（供自评）：**
- 从"过去 vs 现在"的对比切入，画面感强
- 论证两条：便利 + 透明/低门槛，落到具体场景（公交、水电、小贩二维码）
- 让步段（Yet...）指出隐患：冲动消费、老年人被落下
- 结尾落点"普惠"，立意有高度

**易失分点**：只写"方便"一个维度；把 mobile payment 写成抽象的"科技"泛泛而谈；缺具体场景支撑。

## 六、翻译（汉译英）

**原文：** 茶文化在中国有着数千年的历史。中国人不仅把饮茶当作解渴的方式，更把它视为一种修身养性的生活艺术。不同的地区孕育出不同的茶种和冲泡方法，例如西湖龙井以清香著称，而云南普洱则越陈越香。如今，茶已经走出国门，成为连接中国与世界的一座文化桥梁。

**参考译文：**

Tea culture in China has a history of several thousand years. The Chinese regard tea drinking not only as a way to quench thirst but also as a life art of cultivating the mind and character. Different regions have produced different varieties of tea and brewing methods: West Lake Longjing, for example, is famous for its delicate fragrance, while Yunnan Pu'er becomes more fragrant as it ages. Today, tea has gone beyond China's borders and become a cultural bridge connecting China and the rest of the world.

**采分点：**
- "不仅……更……" → not only ... but also ...
- "修身养性" → cultivating the mind and character（意译）
- "越陈越香" → becomes more fragrant as it ages（比较级 + as 从句）
- "走出国门" → has gone beyond China's borders
- "连接中国与世界" → connecting China and the rest of the world（分词作定语）

**易错点**：龙井、普洱等专名用拼音（Longjing / Pu'er）；"越陈越香"是"the + 比较级，the + 比较级"还是"as it ages"——此处用 as it ages 更贴切，避免硬套 the more... the more...。
